# Build jets on the boats' proven foundations

The research supports one clear direction. Phase two should add **arcade-style jets that feel real but stay easy to fly**, built on the systems Boat Race already has: the owner flies its own vehicle, the server decides all damage, the world comes from one height function, and every number sits in config. The jets should not be a flight simulator. The best-reviewed games in this space either fly themselves and lose their thrill, or keep a little real risk (stalling, losing speed in hard turns, hitting the ground) while staying friendly to a mouse or gamepad ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/ace-combat-7-skies-unknown-pc-review); [Skyward FM](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/interview-why485-developer-of-tiny-combat-arena-part-2)). The hardest problem is not graphics or netcode. It is **scale**. The whole race bay is about 1.4 km across and a real-speed jet crosses it in six seconds, so jets need slower game speeds and a much bigger airspace with the existing bays as the low-level set pieces in the middle. About half the boat code (networking, damage, wreck and respawn, the gun's heat, lock-on, flares, the HUD, menus, LAN, audio, garage) carries over. The water physics, the 2D aiming tests and the boat AI do not. The plan below gets a single jet flying first, then an air race (the safest first multiplayer test), then weapons, a dogfight mode and computer pilots. Each stage ends with a check you can run.

## The plan in seven stages, in plain words

Each stage leaves the game working and the boats untouched. The order puts the riskiest unknowns first: whether a jet feels good, and whether the laptop can draw the world from the air. A stage is finished when its check passes, not when the code is written.

| Stage | What you get | Why this order | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Groundwork** | No visible change. The clock gets more precise, boats and jets share one "armed vehicle" base, and the boat-sized limits (200 m ceiling, top speed cap, bay walls) become per-vehicle settings | Jets cannot be added safely until these hidden boat assumptions are lifted | Every existing test still passes: `physics_check.py`, `combat_test.sh`, `net_test.sh`, `smoke_test.sh` |
| **1. One jet that flies** | A jet that starts in the air, flies with keyboard, mouse or gamepad, stalls if flown too slowly, crashes on land or sea, and respawns. A chase camera that rolls with it | Handling is the heart of the game and the biggest guess. Find out early whether it is fun | A new `flight_check.py` passes (stall speed, top speed, turn rate, same result from 20 to 120 fps). You can fly a lap of the bay by hand |
| **2. A sky to fly in** | A larger airspace around the bays, distant hills that fade into haze, sea that looks right from 1–2 km up, simple puff clouds, and a "return to combat area" warning at the edge | Nobody has measured the frame rate from altitude on this laptop. Measure it before building gameplay on it | A photo tour from altitude holds near 60 fps on the Intel laptop |
| **3. Air race** | Fly through rings around a 3D course, alone or against friends on LAN, reusing laps, countdown, results and replay | It tests the jet network code without any shooting. It is also the cheapest real mode | Host and client on two machines see each other's jets smoothly. `net_test.sh` scores rings |
| **4. Weapons in the air** | Nose gun with a lead marker, homing missiles you can dodge by turning hard, flares, a missile warning and clear "who hit me" messages | The weapon code exists but aims only on the flat. It needs to work in 3D | Combat tests pass in 3D. Host and client agree on every hit |
| **5. Dogfight mode** | Free-for-all and two-against-two, with timed rounds, quick air respawns, safe spawns and points shared between damage and kills | Needs flying, world and weapons working first | A four-player LAN round runs start to results with no stuck or wrong scores |
| **6. Computer pilots** | Rival jets that chase, dodge missiles, avoid the ground and come in easy, normal and ace levels | AI copies whatever the player can do, so it comes last | Rivals fill empty slots, never fly into hills and never just joust head-on forever |
| **7. Hangar and polish** | Original jet designs from Blender, paint and upgrades in the garage, jet engine sounds, speed effects, and perhaps a mixed mode where jets hunt the boat race | The look and feel come after the game plays well | Photo tour and environment tests pass. The jets look like nothing that already exists |

Two decisions shape everything else. First, **game speeds are compressed**: a cruise of roughly 400–550 km/h and a boost of 700–900 km/h, not real fighter speeds of 2,000 km/h. At those speeds turns stay a few hundred metres wide and the bays stay useful. Second, **phase two stays original**. It borrows principles from other games, such as "turn hard to dodge a missile" or "a central landmark draws the fight", but not their weapons, names, aircraft, HUD layouts or setting. The jets are fictional airframes built by a new `make_jets.py`, just as the boats are.

## What the genre teaches: risk you can feel, missiles you can beat

Jet combat games sit on a line. At one end are games that "fly themselves", with missiles everywhere, an endless gun and instant respawn. War Thunder's arcade mode, for example, removes stalls, refills ammunition in flight and marks enemies on screen ([War Thunder wiki](https://wiki.warthunder.com/arcade_battles)). In the middle sit hybrids such as Nuclear Option, which models "stalls, spins and g-force effects" yet flies with a mouse "virtual joystick" and holds **95% positive of 9,083 Steam reviews** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2168680)). Full simulators such as DCS are out of scope for a four-player LAN game. The most useful lesson comes from the arcade end's critics. One reviewer called Ace Combat 7 "brilliantly accessible" because it is "so easy to keep your plane in the air", and then wrote that "you have to find your thrills elsewhere" and "there's little challenge" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/ace-combat-7-skies-unknown-pc-review)). The developer of Tiny Combat Arena put the target plainly: "it's far more important that your game feels realistic, rather than actually being realistic" ([Skyward FM](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/interview-why485-developer-of-tiny-combat-arena-part-2)). For Boat Race, that sets the target at **arcade to light hybrid**. Stalling, losing speed in a hard turn and hitting the ground should all be real, learnable risks, but the jet should never be hard to keep in the air.

Assistance should be a layer you can switch off, not a fixed difficulty. H.A.W.X.'s assisted mode made stalling harder and cut damage. Its "Assistance Off" removed the limits and pulled the camera out, and reviewers singled it out: "it feels like you're playing a different game" ([Skyward FM](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/enhanced-reality-system-augmented-reality-flight-assists-in-hawx); [HEXUS](https://hexus.net/gaming/reviews/xbox-360/17564-tom-clancys-hawx-xbox-360-ps3/?page=2)). War Thunder's mouse control works through an "Instructor" that flies the plane towards where you point and stops the wing reaching a stalling angle ([War Thunder](https://warthunder.com/en/news/4366-wiki-article-how-the-instructor-works-en)). A 2026 Star Fox remake gives the signature moves, a somersault and a U-turn, one button each ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/preview/2026/05/30/pretty-smooth-flying)). The recommendation follows directly. The default is **"Assisted"**: wings level themselves, a limiter prevents stalls, the ground warns you, and one-button U-turn and loop are available. **"Expert"** removes the limiter and allows drifting past the stall, with a wider camera.

The weapon loop is where arcade games most often go wrong, in both directions. Ace Combat 7's player guide says most missile lock cones are "bigger than your screen". It also says missiles "will almost never hit from the side unless you're going under 600 kph", and that the best counter is to keep turning the same way, because "reversing or barrel rolling helps the missile track" ([AC7 multiplayer guide](https://rentry.org/acegMPguide)). War Thunder's forums argue both "are flares too powerful?" and "does missile lethality need a rethink?" ([WT forum](https://forum.warthunder.com/t/are-flares-too-powerful/261161); [WT forum](https://forum.warthunder.com/t/does-missile-lethality-need-a-rethink/253441)). The hybrid answer is to make missiles **slower, shorter-ranged and fewer, so they can be outflown**: "missiles slower so that they're easier to see and evade kinematically", with emphasis on "close range gun kills" ([Skyward FM](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/interview-why485-developer-of-tiny-combat-arena-part-2)). War Thunder's own evasion advice pairs flares *with* a hard turn and lower throttle, because heat signature follows thrust ([WT wiki: flares](https://wiki.warthunder.com/mechanics/5026-aerial-infrared-countermeasures-flares); [WT wiki: avoiding missiles](https://wiki.warthunder.com/212-avoiding-air-to-air-missiles)). For Boat Race the loop should be: an **endless nose gun limited by heat** (the deck gun already works this way), a **small missile rack that refills over time**, and flares on a cooldown that only work if you also turn. Missiles should hit from behind and miss from the side at speed, so getting on someone's tail matters more than spamming lock-on.

Clear feedback matters as much as balance. "It's never fun dying in a game without knowing what happened, or if it was even your fault" ([Skyward FM](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/interview-why485-developer-of-tiny-combat-arena-part-2)). Reviewers praise "the glee in wheeling away to safety, your target a burning wreckage behind you" and "following an enemy plane into a steep dive" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/ace-combat-7-skies-unknown-pc-review)). They complain about repetition, too little challenge and weak AI allies ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/project-wingman-review)). For a small lobby, Ace Combat 7's multiplayer scoring is a useful principle: it gives most of a kill's value for damage dealt (up to 70%) and the rest for the final blow, which rewards assists and softens kill-stealing ([AC7 multiplayer guide](https://rentry.org/acegMPguide)). Boat Race should use its own split, tuned in config. The research found no sourced figures for match lengths or respawn timers, so the suggested five-minute rounds and three-second respawns are starting guesses to test by hand.

## Flight model: steer by target turn rates, as the boat already does

The proven cheap model for this kind of game is the "semi-arcade" design that Vazgriz documented: "fake as much as possible" rather than simulate airflow ([Vazgriz, Part 1](https://vazgriz.com/346/flight-simulator-in-unity3d-part-1/)). Thrust pushes along the nose. Drag grows with speed squared. Lift comes from a hand-drawn curve of **lift against angle of attack (the angle between the nose and the direction of travel)** that peaks at about 30° and falls off beyond it, which is the stall. A second drag term grows with the square of lift, so **hard turns bleed speed** and a natural best turning speed emerges. Crucially, the stick does not move simulated control surfaces. It sets a **target rotation rate**, and the model pushes the body's actual rotation towards it at a capped rate. A **G limiter** then scales the input down so a full pull cannot exceed the limit. Vazgriz shows that scaling the input, rather than clipping it, avoids a dead zone where more stick does nothing ([Vazgriz, Part 1](https://vazgriz.com/346/flight-simulator-in-unity3d-part-1/)). This is close to how `ABoatPawn` already steers. Its yaw torque is `(DesiredYawRate − CurrentYawRate) · YawInertia · TurnResponse` (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:699-781`). The jet uses the same idea on three axes.

The heavier alternatives are not worth it. JSBSim has an Unreal plugin that Epic's simulation team started, but it is simulator-grade, LGPL-licensed, and users report high-speed oscillation that needed a fixed 120 Hz step ([JSBSim README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JSBSim-Team/jsbsim/master/README.md); [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/combat-flight-simulator-prototype-in-unreal-5-jsbsim/569418)). Chaos Vehicles has an aerofoil array ([UE API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/ChaosVehicles/FVehicleAerofoilConfig)), but it would add a plugin, against the project's rule of no new plugins without good reason, and no thrust-driven aircraft tutorial for it was found.

Scale is the deciding numerical fact. Turn rate is g·√(n²−1)/V, so a 9 G turn at 150 m/s gives about 33°/s with a 256 m radius, while at 300 m/s the radius is about 1 km (standard physics, and consistent with the roughly 21–25°/s cited for F-16s in [BoltFlight](https://boltflight.com/f-16-turn-rate-a-detailed-analysis-of-maneuverability/) and a [DCS forum test](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/280462-dcs-f16-turn-performance/page/5), both weak sources). The playable bay is only **1.44 × 1.28 km** (`BoatTrack::BoundsX/Y`, `Source/BoatRace/BoatTrack.h:81-88`). At 250 m/s a jet crosses it in 5.8 s, and a 3 G turn at that speed is wider than the whole bay. Hence the compressed speeds in the plan. Suggested starting values are a stall near 180–220 km/h, cruise 400–550 km/h, boost 700–900 km/h, a player G limit of 7–9, instantaneous turn of 25–35°/s near the best turning speed, and a roll rate of 180–270°/s. These are design judgements to tune in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`, not sourced figures. Roll rates that high also exceed the boat's hard cap of `MaxSpinDegreesPerSecond = 190` (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:42`), which must become per-vehicle.

### Integrate the jet yourself rather than hand it to Chaos

The boat adds forces once per frame and relies on engine substepping (`bSubstepping=True`, 16.7 ms, up to 6 steps in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`), with `physics_check.py` proving frame-rate independence offline. That has worked for a 29 m/s boat. Two findings argue against repeating it for a jet. Community reports say classic substepping is effectively a no-op under Chaos, and that the alternative, async physics tick, is mutually exclusive with substepping and has crash and force-scaling reports ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/relationship-between-tick-physics-async-and-substepping/2395435); [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/add-force-works-strange-in-async-tick-physics/1924807); [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/async-physics-crashes/2601520)). And Chaos's continuous collision "will take any opportunity to bail out" and can still tunnel ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/chaos-contact-modification-behaves-oddly-with-ccd/2689496)). The recommended design is for the owning machine to **integrate the flight model itself at a fixed 1/120 s step with an accumulator**, then move the jet with a swept `SetActorLocationAndRotation`. The sweep catches thin obstacles such as crane booms and the canyon arch. Ground and sea contact are tested analytically against `BayTerrain::GetHeight` and `OceanWaves`, as the missile already does. This matters because the outer terrain ring has **no collision at all** (`BuildSection(1, Outer, false)`, `Source/BoatRace/BayTerrain.cpp:262-275`). This design keeps the "owner simulates, others hold a kinematic copy" rule, keeps Chaos away from a 150 m/s body, and makes the Python mirror (`flight_check.py`, modelled on `physics_check.py`) an exact copy of the C++ maths. The cost is that jets do not shove each other physically, which jet games rarely need. The UE 5.9 names of the async-tick API could not be checked against local headers, which is another reason to avoid depending on it.

### Three control schemes feeding one set of target rates

The controller should offer three schemes, all producing the same normalised pitch, roll and yaw commands that the G limiter then scales. **Mouse aim** follows the open-source MouseFlight approach: the mouse moves an aim direction relative to the camera, and an autopilot steers the nose onto it, banking into large turns and using yaw for fine aim ([MouseFlight](https://github.com/brihernandez/MouseFlight); [Vazgriz, Part 3](https://vazgriz.com/503/creating-a-flight-simulator-in-unity3d-part-3/)). A proportional-derivative loop (proportional on angle error, damping from the measured rotation rate) is enough, because the flight model already tracks rates. **Gamepad Assisted** turns with the left stick by banking and pulling automatically, the convention Ace Combat calls "Standard" ([GamePressure](https://gamepressure.com/ace-combat-7/standard-and-expert-handling-modes/zabcd1)). **Gamepad Expert and keyboard** map stick or keys directly to roll and pitch, with yaw on the shoulders or Q/E. Keyboard inputs should ramp up over a fraction of a second so a tapped key does not snap to full rate. The project polls keys directly in `ABoatPawn::ReadInput` and has no Enhanced Input (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:364-470`). Keeping that style with a per-vehicle `ReadInput` is simpler than migrating, though mouse aim needs mouse deltas while flying, which the boat never used. **Skip takeoff and landing.** Start in the air from a formation grid at cruise speed after the existing countdown, and treat ground or water contact as a crash. Landing needs gear, wheel friction and low-speed lift aids, which commercial assets sell as a feature in their own right ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ritzredemption-fighter-jet-flight-physics-playable-jet-with-takeoff-landing/2838478)).

## Netcode: keep the boat's model, but send twice as often and stop guessing in straight lines

The boat's network design suits jets on a LAN with friends. Shipped flight games keep hits and damage server-decided while clients draw predicted visuals. War Thunder simulates every shell on both server and client from the same start time, but "a client cannot turn a miss into a hit" ([War Thunder devblog](https://warthunder.com/en/news/5005-development-shell-and-bullet-synchronization-en)). Boat Race already works this way: owners send their state 30 times a second through an unreliable RPC, the server checks it and forwards it, remote copies are kinematic, and the server alone applies damage (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:849-945`, `1006-1044`). Unreal 5.9's newer options do not fit. Resimulation is still labelled "Work In Progress" and its benefit is physical contact, which jets rarely have ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networked-physics-overview); [EPhysicsReplicationMode API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/EPhysicsReplicationMode)). Mover and ChaosMover are only aiming for Beta during 2026 ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/question-about-the-next-steps-for-chaos-mover/2743117)). The Network Prediction Plugin is "not currently under active development beyond general support and bug fixes" ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/status-of-network-prediction-plugin-looking-for-epic-response/502509)). Iris is beta and off by default ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/5-7-iris-strategy/2699685)). **Leave all four alone.**

What has to change is how remote jets are drawn. Today a remote boat is dead-reckoned in a straight line for up to **0.3 s**, then eased with `VInterpTo` (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:40`, `927-945`). At 150 m/s that allows 45 m of straight-line guessing on a jet that is actually turning. DCS players have complained for years that "extreme high-G manoeuvres result in the plane warping for everyone else" ([DCS forum](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/257345-extreme-high-g-maneuvers-result-in-the-plane-warping-for-everyone-else-in-the-game)), which is consistent with that kind of naive prediction. Gaffer on Games recommends an interpolation buffer of about three packet intervals, Hermite curves using the sent velocity (linear interpolation "jitters"), slerp for rotation, and extrapolation only over short windows of about 50–250 ms ([Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)). For jets, that means raising the send rate to **45–60 Hz**, buffering about two to three packets (roughly 35–70 ms on LAN), extrapolating at most 100–150 ms using **constant turn rate rather than straight lines**, and blending errors away over 100–200 ms instead of snapping. The bandwidth is trivial. About 30 bytes per update at 60 Hz is roughly 2–4 KB/s per jet per receiver, a back-of-envelope estimate rather than a measurement.

Weapons follow the existing split. The gun stays server-decided, with only "trigger held" replicated and tracers drawn locally (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:1308-1416`). On LAN the server's view of a target is one or two snapshots old, about 4–8 m at 250 m/s, so a slightly generous hit volume covers it. Head-on passes at 500 m/s closing speed are the exception, where 20 ms of staleness is 10 m. Server rewind (keeping a second of position history and testing shots against where the target was) is only needed if the game ever goes onto the internet. Missiles should remain server-flown. Their **launch** (launcher, target, time, random seed) should be replicated so every machine can run the same guidance locally from the shared clock, with a low-rate position correction, and a server seed should decide which flare a missile chases. This follows the project's rule that anything derivable from the clock is never sent. Two boat-only limits in `ServerSendMove` would silently break jets: **height is clamped to −20…200 m and speed to 2.5× the boat's top speed** (about 72 m/s) (`Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:893-900`). The server clock is also a `float` (`Source/BoatRace/BoatRaceGameState.cpp:96-99`). After about 18 hours of uptime its step is about 8 ms, which is 2–8 m of error for anything at jet or bullet speed integrated from the clock. Making it `double` is Stage 0's first job. The usual caveat remains: multiplayer has only ever been checked on one machine, so Stage 3's two-machine air race is also the first real Wi-Fi test.

## Computer pilots: a priority stack over one shared steering controller

Shipped and indie flight AI uses layered state machines or decision trees over simple steering, not machine learning. NASA's rule-based Adaptive Maneuvering Logic orders its decisions as ground avoidance, then pointing, then lead or lag manoeuvres, then missile evasion ([NASA CR-4160](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19890018022/downloads/19890018022.pdf)). The DARPA AlphaDogfight winner beat an F-16 instructor 5–0, but only after training on at least **4 billion samples**, guns only, one against one ([The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/ai-versus-ace)), which is impractical here. The indie devlog for Squadron: Mercenaries is the most useful model. Its pilots run Mission, then Operation, then reusable Tasks. Engagement uses a data-driven decision tree of named manoeuvres. Difficulty restricts which manoeuvres a pilot may use. And its first AI fell into **"space jousting"**, endless head-on passes, the classic failure ([Squadron: Mercenaries devlog](https://ibgoge.itch.io/wing-mercenaries/devlog/718838/recapturing-the-golden-era-of-dogfighting-behind-the-scenes-of-squadron-mercenaries-space-combat-ai)). A simpler indie AI breaks off on a timer and fires only when the target is roughly ahead and in range ([jli69](https://jli69.github.io/blog/2024/09/05/flight-and-fight-plane-ai.html)).

The recommended rival jet has five layers. Safety comes first: look 3–5 s ahead along the velocity against `BayTerrain::GetHeight`, which is cheaper than ray traces, and pull up in proportion to time to impact. Threat response comes second: on a missile warning, turn to put the missile off the wingtip, drop flares on a timer, and break hard just before impact. Tactics come third: score targets by distance, angle and "who is shooting at me", with hysteresis so the AI does not flip between them. Manoeuvre comes fourth: chase the lead point inside gun range, a point behind the target when closing too fast, and dive to regain speed when slow. Last is a PD flight controller that sets the same pitch, roll, yaw and throttle commands a player would, just as `ABoatRivalController` drives the boat through `SetDriverInput` (`Source/BoatRace/BoatRivalController.h:20-40`). The mouse-aim autopilot and the AI share that controller. DCS gives its aircraft types a preference for "one-circle" or "two-circle" turning fights ([DCS newsletter](https://www.digitalcombatsimulator.com/en/news/newsletters/d48111b832efd4f57df12df8f46e7ca1)), a cheap way to give each jet class a distinct personality. Difficulty should come from reaction time (about 0.2–1.0 s), aim error, flare discipline and the allowed manoeuvre set, never from visible physics cheats. A break-off rule after every head-on pass prevents jousting. The existing boat AI is a 2D waypoint follower and is not reusable beyond its controller shell.

## World and performance: an outer airspace around the bays, drawn with rings, fog and puffs

Air-combat map sizes vary widely. War Thunder's arcade-only maps are "32 km x 32 km or less" and its general air maps 64–128 km ([War Thunder wiki](https://wiki.warthunder.com/arcade_battles)). Those hold 16–32 aircraft. Sky Combat 2, an arena game, uses about **25–30 km²**, roughly 5 km square, sized by how long pilots take to reach key places, retreat and rejoin ([LL Games](https://ll-games.com/en/news/interview-with-a-level-designer/)). With two to four players at compressed speeds, a playable airspace of about **8–10 km square** with the existing bay at its centre is a sound starting point. This is a judgement, not a sourced figure. The Sky Combat 2 designer's principles fit Boat Race's scenery well. A central landmark should draw the fighting. Spawns should sit at equal distances around it. Pilots should always be able to climb or dive to escape. Tight corridors and camping spots should be avoided, but cliffs, bridges and towers make good cover, and tunnel-like routes let players break contact. Obstacles must read by shape and colour at speed. The common failure is "creating the same open-field map over and over again" ([LL Games](https://ll-games.com/en/news/interview-with-a-level-designer/)). Canyon Run's 65–87 m sandstone walls make a natural low-level run, the harbour's cranes make strafing ground, and the tropical bay's sea stacks make cover. That keeps the project's rule that the three circuits must look different. Ace Combat shows that clouds can be gameplay without volumetric rendering. Entering a cloud can briefly break a lock and slows locking, while targets stay on the HUD ([Skyward FM](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/the-form-and-function-of-clouds-and-weather-in-ace-combat-7)). In Boat Race that becomes a sphere test on the existing `CanLock` path. The boundary should be a soft "return to combat area" warning and timer, like Ace Combat 7's Return Line ([Ace Combat wiki](https://acecombat.wiki.gg/wiki/Long_Day)), not the boat's spring wall.

Performance is the largest unknown, because **nobody has measured this laptop with the camera above 200 m**. The server clamp has meant nothing has ever flown there. Today the land mesh stops at about 5.2 km, the outer ring has 60 m cells and no collision, and the camera-following water grid is only 280 m across on the low preset, so its edge will show from altitude (`Source/BoatRace/BayTerrain.cpp:22-25`, `Source/BoatRace/WaterSurface.cpp:31-36`). The research points to cheap techniques that fit the code-only design. The first is a **geometry clipmap**: nested rings of procedural mesh, each with double the cell size of the one inside, following the camera in whole cells, as `WaterSurface` already does, with heights from the same `BayTerrain` function. This avoids World Partition, Landscape and HLOD, which assume editor-built maps and draw forum bug reports ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-do-i-build-correct-landscape-hlods-in-world-partition/527124)). The water grid's cell size should grow with camera height. The second is Sky Atmosphere's lookup-table **aerial perspective** with exponential height fog to hide the far edge. The fast paths (`r.SkyAtmosphere.FastSkyLUT=1`, `AerialPerspectiveLUT.FastApplyOnOpaque=1`) are supported on low-end PCs, and raising Aerial Perspective View Distance Scale thickens haze seen from altitude ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sky-atmosphere-component-in-unreal-engine)). The third is **billboard puff clouds**, the technique Microsoft Flight Simulator 2004 used before volumetrics ([SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/realistic-and-fast-cloud-rendering-in-computer-games); [Harris GDC 2002](https://aurora.cs.uaf.edu/~olawlor/academic/thesis/ref/RTCloudsForGames_HarrisGDC2002.pdf)), placed from a seed and the shared clock, since volumetric clouds are already off in the low preset. Intel's UE5 guide adds raising the scalability thresholds for modern integrated GPUs, avoiding vertex animation and masked materials on low settings, cutting material count, and measuring only after the machine has warmed up and throttled ([Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/unreal-engine-optimization-chapter-2.html)). Since UE 5.4, large-world rendering no longer jitters at tile boundaries, provided materials use Camera Relative World Position ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/large-world-coordinates-rendering-in-unreal-engine-5)). A world of ±10 km is safe.

Speed has to be felt as well as flown. Reported cues are a wider field of view, objects of known size near the ground, and speed lines and shake that grow on boost ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/maneuverability-in-games-the-importance-of-speed-and-control); [AVSIM forum](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/499872-what-helps-with-the-sensation-of-speed-on-landing/)). The evidence is anecdotal. The boat game's pooled puffs, wake and spray can supply air streaks, wingtip trails and water spray when skimming low, all cheap on integrated graphics. At several kilometres a jet is smaller than a pixel, so the HUD target box *is* the target. The canvas HUD gains a lead marker, 3D lock brackets (today's show 2D distance, `Source/BoatRace/BoatRaceHUD.cpp:1170-1200`), off-screen arrows for enemies and incoming missiles, altitude, a horizon line, a G and stall warning, and a cause-of-death line.

## How much of the code carries over

About half of `ABoatPawn`'s 2,405 lines are not about water. The networking (`FBoatNetState`, `ServerSendMove`, teleports), damage, wreck and revive, the gun's clock-driven fire rate with heat and jamming, lock progress, flares, hit feedback and loadout plumbing should move into a shared `AArmedVehiclePawn` base, with `ABoatPawn` and a new `AJetPawn` beneath it. That is cheaper than renaming the 228 `ABoatPawn` references across 15 files. The 8 `TActorIterator<ABoatPawn>` loops, the HUD and the blast code then switch to the base type. The blast damage path is already 3D (`FVector::Dist`, `Source/BoatRace/BoatProjectile.cpp:66-130`). The missile's segment-based proximity fuse already handles fast closure. The aiming tests are not 3D. `CanLock` uses a 2D ±45° cone, `FindGunTarget` a 2D bearing, and hits are a probability roll (`Source/BoatRace/BoatWeapons.cpp:332-369`, `Source/BoatRace/BoatPawn.cpp:1271-1362`). The missile flies at **65 m/s with pure pursuit and is clamped to sea-skim height**, so it could never catch a jet. Jets need 3D cones, proportional-navigation guidance with a G limit so missiles can be outturned, a computed lead for the gun, and a coarser terrain check in `HasClearShot`. Its 5 m sampling would cost 400–600 height lookups per check at jet ranges. The game mode, kill feed, `Hits`/`Wrecks` tally, countdown, results, replay, menus-as-data, LAN discovery (bump its signature version so boat and jet builds do not cross-join), prefs, coins, garage structure and synthesised audio all extend by adding data.

## Conclusion

The research changes the picture of phase two in one important way. The expensive-sounding parts, flight physics and fast-aircraft netcode, are solved problems at this scale, and Boat Race's existing patterns (rate-target steering, owner-simulated vehicles, server-decided damage, analytic world, Python mirrors of the physics) are the right ones to extend. The real risks are hidden assumptions that boats never stressed: a 200 m ceiling, a 72 m/s speed cap, a float clock, flat aiming, a terrain ring with no collision, and a world drawn for a camera that never leaves the water. That is why the plan spends its first stage on groundwork and its second on measuring the view from the air, before any combat.

What remains unknown should guide the testing. There are no frame-rate measurements from altitude on the Meteor Lake laptop. No sourced figures exist for arcade lock times, missile speeds, respawn timers or match lengths. Two-machine Wi-Fi play has never been tried. And, as with the boats, handling is a guess until someone flies it by hand. The air race in Stage 3 is the most valuable early milestone. It is cheap, it is fun on its own, it proves the jet netcode on real Wi-Fi without the complication of weapons, and it gives the project a playable flying mode even if dogfighting later needs more tuning than expected.
